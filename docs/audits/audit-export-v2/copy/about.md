About

I’m interested in the part of AI that breaks after the demo.

I’m Favour Diokpo, an AI product systems designer. The impressive part of an AI product
is easy to show. I care about what comes next — memory, permissions, files, trust,
handoff, cost, and control — the parts that quietly decide whether people keep
using it.

My work sits where AI behavior meets human understanding. I’m most useful when a
product is powerful but fragile: agents that act invisibly, permissions that interrupt,
memory and files that disappear, spend with no receipt. I turn that into interfaces
people can read, control, and rely on .

I come from enterprise internal tools at Ubisoft, where I’m a Senior UX Specialist on
platforms that help studios plan work, request hires, and make approvals — tools that sit at
the intersection of money, people, and game development , used by more than a
thousand people. My day-to-day there is turning messy, policy-heavy workflows into something
people can actually use without babysitting the system.

That work taught me how to make dense, high-stakes software feel calm and trustworthy, and
how to turn ambiguity into decisions people can act on. I bring the same discipline to AI:
name the system first, then design the surface.

How I think
Six things I believe about AI UX.
The demo is the easy part
AI products break after the demo — in memory, permissions, files, handoff, cost, and control. That’s the part I design for.

Invisible behavior is a bug
If an agent acted, saved, forgot, or spent money, the user should be able to see it. Hidden state is where trust dies.

Trust is a design decision
Not a vibe. It’s made of concrete states — visible causes, reversible actions, honest empty states, and no fake success.

Systems before screens
I name the objects and states a product needs first. Good pixels follow a good model; they can’t rescue a bad one.

Density can be calm
Enterprise tools taught me that dense, high-stakes software can still feel legible — if the hierarchy is honest.

Write the spec, then break it
I ship specs and then QA the states most teams leak: ghost turns, duplicate messages, phantom attachments.

I make AI systems easier to trust, control, and actually use.
