## Context

At **Ubisoft**, planning and staffing decisions ran through internal tools used by more than **1,000 people** across production. Over years of incremental additions, the surfaces had fragmented — dozens of one-off modals, inconsistent approval flows, and quota rules that were hard to see and easy to misread.

## Stat chips

- **1,000+** — Users across teams
- **40+** — Modals audited
- **1** — Coherent system
- **3** — Workflow domains

## Problem

The tools worked, but people didn't trust them. Approvals felt opaque, quota visibility was buried, and performance-aware data was scattered across surfaces that behaved differently. When a system that governs staffing feels unpredictable, people route around it — and governance quietly breaks.

## My role

**UX designer for internal tools**, leading the audit and redesign in partnership with product, engineering, and operations. I owned the research, the systemization, and the governance/workflow improvements — from messy inventory to a coherent interaction model.

## Research

- Inventoried and mapped **40+ modals** to expose duplication and inconsistency.
- Traced real approval and quota workflows to find where trust broke down.
- Interviewed cross-functional users to separate policy problems from interface problems.

Image: Screenshot placeholder — Enterprise — 40+ modal audit map

## Design systems & process

- Consolidated one-off modals into consistent, reusable patterns.
- Made quota visibility a first-class part of the interface, not a buried number.
- Designed approval flows with clear status, ownership, and next steps.
- Built performance-aware data interfaces that stayed readable under real density.

Callout (quote): In enterprise tools, trust is a feature. If people can't see why a decision happened, they stop believing the system.

## Governance & workflow improvements

By aligning the interface with how governance actually needed to work, approvals became traceable, quotas became visible at the point of decision, and the same rules read the same way everywhere. Cross-functional alignment turned a pile of modals into a system people could reason about.

## Impact

- A fragmented tool set became one coherent, trustworthy system.
- Approval and quota workflows became legible to the people who depend on them.
- Consistency reduced confusion and the workarounds that erode governance.

## What this demonstrates

- Taming complex, high-stakes enterprise UX at real scale.
- Systemizing chaos — turning 40+ modals into one coherent model.
- Designing for governance, approvals, and performance-aware data.
- Driving cross-functional alignment across product, engineering, and operations.
